A FIGHTING GAME ABOUT TIMING

Every fighter is a stick figure and every fight is a read. You get a direction and four buttons, one per limb, and no motion inputs to memorize. What wins is pressing the right button at the right moment: wait for the other player to commit, then make them pay for it.

Mashing loses. Every swing costs stamina, spending the last of it leaves you fainted and wide open, and a press thrown where it doesn't belong comes out as a stumble. The animation aims at Xiao Xiao, the old stick-figure fight cartoons, and the office tower is lit like a modern 2D game. It's free, it runs in your browser, and the source is on GitHub.

WHAT'S IN THE GAME

FIGHTERS

From the TEMP on day one to the corner office. Every fighter has their own stats, body, unique moves and strings, with a full move sheet on their page.

ARCADE LADDER

stages up the building, best of three, against CPUs that each fight their own way: the turtle, the rusher, the grappler. Beat the boss without a continue and you'll find out who else is upstairs.

HORDE FLOORS

Wave after wave of goons through the office doors. Clear the floor, vault the stairwell railing, and do it again one floor down. See how deep you get.

ONLINE VERSUS

Send a friend a four-letter code or a link. A crew room is a permanent link for your group, with standings, head-to-head records and winner stays on. Rematch with one press.

ROLLBACK NETCODE

Both machines run the whole fight and quietly fix their guesses when the other player's input arrives, the way the big fighting games do it. Peer to peer whenever the two devices can reach each other.

BOTCHES AND SECRETS

Mash and you'll mess up: overbalance, spin out, trip over your own feet. A few mistimed presses, in just the right window, come out as something much better. We're not saying which.

PHONES AND CONTROLLERS

Touch controls with a floating stick and four arcade buttons, any standard gamepad (Xbox, PlayStation, 8BitDo), or the keyboard. The game switches its button hints to whatever you pick up.

INSTALLABLE

Add it to your home screen and it opens full screen in landscape, starts fast and plays offline. New versions show up on their own.

HOW TO PLAY

One button per limb, like Tekken, from your fighter's own point of view.

1LEFT PUNCHjab · key U
2RIGHT PUNCHcross · key I
3LEFT KICKsnap kick · key J
4RIGHT KICKroundhouse · key K

Add a direction for more: down and a button for the low attacks and the uppercut, toward and a button for the body blow, the lunge and the overhead.

GUARD
Hold back, away from the other fighter. You walk back, and your guard snaps up on its own when an attack is coming that can reach you. Standing guard stops highs and mids. Lows go under it, so hold down and back for those.
PARRY
Tap toward the other fighter just before a hit lands, down-forward for a low. Nothing gets through and they reel long enough to eat a jab. It has to be a fresh tap: holding forward, dashing or mashing never parries, and a tap that guesses wrong leaves you walking into the hit.
THROWS
1+3 or 2+4 up close. A throw beats a guard but whiffs on someone crouching. Press the same two buttons while you're grabbed to break it.
STAMINA
The thin bar under your health is your attack budget. Every swing spends some, and it refills fast once you stop swinging.
FAINT
Spend the last point and you faint: out on your feet for a beat, no guard, wide open. Getting knocked down fills the bar back up.
KNOCKED DOWN
You stay down until you choose. Up stands in place, left or right rolls, and a button strikes from the floor: 1 or 3 is a mid kick, 2 or 4 a low sweep. They can't be hit until they swing, but they're easy to punish if blocked. Do nothing and you stand after a second.
STOMP
Down and a kick over a fighter on the floor stomps them for real damage. Land it and you have time to guard their wake-up strike. Whiff it, when they roll or strike up through it, and you're the one wide open.
AIR RECOVERY
Juggled or launched, press a button once the hits stop and you flip upright and land on your feet. Press right as the window opens and you come out with a jab. Press too early and you're locked out, so mashing never saves you.
STRINGS
Press in rhythm, as the last hit finishes, and the hits chain: 1, 2, 3, 4 is the Neon Tornado. Mashing doesn't chain. Every fighter's strings are on their page.
SOUND
Browsers keep a page quiet until you touch it, so the first click, tap or key turns the sound on. M turns the music off and on, N skips a track.

THE CONTROLS

PLAY A FRIEND ONLINE

No accounts and nothing to download. From the main menu pick VERSUS ONLINE, choose your fighter, and then one of these.

QUICK MATCH

  1. One of you picks HOST A GAME and gets a four-letter room code and a link.
  2. Send the link, or read the code out loud. Codes skip 0, O, 1, I and L so nobody mixes them up.
  3. The other opens the link, or picks JOIN A GAME and types the code.

When a match ends, REMATCH keeps the same fighters and NEW CHARACTERS goes back to the pick. The room keeps a running tally until somebody leaves.

CREW ROOMS

  1. Pick CREATE CREW ROOM and name your crew. You get a link that stays good as long as people keep using it.
  2. Drop it in the group chat. Everybody who opens it lands in the crew's lobby.
  3. Press PLAY when you're ready. The next two ready players fight, and the winner stays on.

The lobby keeps standings for the whole crew: wins, losses, your record against each person, and everyone's favorite fighter.

WHY IT FEELS LOCAL

Online fights use rollback netcode. Each side plays its own input right away, guesses the other's, and quietly rewinds and replays the last few frames when the guess was wrong. The connection goes peer to peer when it can, and through Cloudflare when two phones can't reach each other.

Want to keep your crews on a new phone? PROFILE in the online menu saves them to your email.
